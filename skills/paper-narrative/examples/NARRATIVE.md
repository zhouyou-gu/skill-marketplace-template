# Paper Narrative

## Title, Abstract, and Keywords

1. **Title:** Identify the question of whether an explanation established in one setting carries over to another.

2. **Abstract:** Introduce the reason to expect the explanation to carry over, then state how the paper tests that expectation.

   - Close with the answer supported by the comparison.

3. **Keywords:** Use the paper’s established terms for the phenomenon and the approach used to study it.

## I. Introduction

4. **Paragraph 1, motivation:** Explain why the original explanation is useful and why readers might rely on it in a second setting.

5. **Paragraph 2, open question:** Identify the condition that changes between the two settings and explain why that change puts the original reasoning in doubt.

   - End with the question the comparison must resolve.

## II. Analysis

6. **Paragraph 1, basis for comparison:** Explain what the two settings have in common before introducing the difference on which the argument depends.

7. **Paragraph 2, interpretation:** Explain what the comparison establishes about the proposed explanation.

   - Distinguish the part of the explanation tested by this comparison from the part that remains open.

## III. Conclusion

8. **Paragraph 1, answer:** Return to the opening question and state how far the reader can carry the explanation beyond its original setting.
